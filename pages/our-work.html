OUR MISSION

Rethinking energy systems for a cleaner, fairer, and climate-resilient future.

Our mission is to advance a just energy transition by exposing the structural drivers of Pakistan’s gas and fossil-fuel dependence; challenging subsidies, governance failures, and entrenched industrial interests, especially captive power plants—that distort markets, deepen inequality, and worsen the climate crisis. CAEA works to democratize climate and energy discourse, center community experiences, and advocate for people-centric, rights-based policies across water, energy, and climate systems. Through evidence-based research, public mobilization, and strategic policy engagement, we strengthen national capacity to phase out gas from industrial and domestic sectors, counter fossil-fuel intensity in power and industry, and push for transparent, least-cost, and climate-just pathways that prioritize public health, economic stability, and intergenerational equity.

FOSSIL FUEL PHASEOUT

Rethinking energy systems for a cleaner, fairer, and climate-resilient future.

CAEA situates the phase-out of gas as an urgent political and ecological imperative, not just policy preference. Pakistan’s deepening dependence on natural gas and imported LNG has locked the country into extractive and financially ruinous value chains, producing large-scale losses through chronic inefficiencies such as unaccounted-for gas (UFG), fiscal leakages, and long-term contractual liabilities. These economic harms are inseparable from gas’s climate violence: methane emissions across the entire gas chain - from upstream extraction and processing to pipelines, turning the local ecology into sacrifice zones, LNG terminals, and downstream power generation - act as deadly accelerants to an already catastrophic climate crisis that Pakistan, among the least responsible yet most exposed, continues to endure. Our analysis proceeds through a political-ecological and value-chain lens, combining infrastructure mapping, regulatory scrutiny, and climate justice frameworks to interrogate gas as a system of power rather than a neutral fuel.

We identify the power sector as the primary and most strategic site for gas phase-out. Imported LNG, far from delivering energy security, has rendered electricity structurally unaffordable while deepening dependence on volatile international markets. As distributed solar rapidly expands across Pakistan and demand is increasingly squeezed, LNG and domestic gas alike become economically irrational - stranded assets that inflate tariffs, undermine grid stability, and intensify Pakistan’s emissions profile without delivering social benefit. In this context, gas no longer functions as a “transition fuel” but as an obstacle to an equitable and viable energy future.

CAEA recognizes that gas extraction, transmission, and distribution - from upstream fields to downstream urban networks - constitute deeply entrenched political and institutional terrains.

CAEA remains actively engaged in advancing demand reduction, alternatives, and public contestation. For us, gas phase-out is not merely a technical adjustment but a struggle to confront fossil dependency, reclaim policy space from extractive interests, and align Pakistan’s energy system with ecological limits, social justice, and climate survival.

CLIMATE JUSTICE

Ecological democracy, gender equity & frontline communities' sovereignty over resources

At CAEA, we imagine climate justice beyond merely reducing emissions, compensating losses or making infrastructure resilient. We understand it as the task of refusing the conversion of living landscapes into empty, measurable and extractable space. Every ecology is also a world of knowledge, livelihood, memory, movement and social relations. Justice, therefore, asks who has the power to define a landscape’s problems, whose knowledge is recognised, who receives the benefits of an intervention and who is made to carry its violence. Climate justice means defending diverse ecologies from dispossession and restoring communities’ authority to adapt and steward the worlds that sustain them.

Pakistan inhabits different ecological worlds who are victims of the climate crises. Through intentional research, storytelling, and making a dialogic space, CAEA intends to centre the voices of communities victim of the climate crises. Part of this work involves closely examining the social and environmental costs of large infrastructure projects, costs that are often overlooked in official reports and government decisions.

Our goal is to build narrative, shape climate conversations, and support creative reflective spaces to imagine just future that place communities, sustainability, and justice at the center of climate action.

FAST FASHION

Supply chain transparency, industrial captive power emissions & fair worker transitions

The fashion industry today is built on deep-rooted injustice. What was once rooted in craft and creativity has become a system driven by fast, cheap consumption — and someone always pays the price.

Fast fashion runs on a global chain of exploitation. To meet demand from wealthier countries, factories in places like Pakistan push workers to their limits under poor and unsafe conditions. This exploitation also plays out locally: resources and labor move from less-developed regions to more industrialized ones, often with little fair compensation. Raw materials drawn from these regions rarely benefit the communities they come from.

Our work traces this entire supply chain — from where raw materials are extracted, to where garments are made, to where they are sold — to expose these hidden patterns of extraction and inequality. By sharing the stories of the people and places usually left out of the conversation, we aim to build momentum for a fairer, more equitable alternative to the current system.
